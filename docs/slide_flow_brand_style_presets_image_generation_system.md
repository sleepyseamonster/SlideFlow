# SlideFlow Brand Style Presets – Image Generation System

## Purpose of This Document

This document defines the **core Style presets** used across SlideFlow for image generation, SlideBoard backgrounds, and brand-consistent visual output.

These styles are not cosmetic themes. They are **behavioral presets** that quietly control how background images are generated, how color palettes are applied, and how much visual weight is given to supporting elements versus foreground content.

Each style is intentionally designed to:
- Stay text-safe and overlay-friendly
- Respect the user’s selected color palette
- Scale across carousels, captions, and future visual features
- Produce predictable, brand-aligned results

The three foundational styles are:
- Minimal
- Bold
- Elegant

---

## Style 1: Minimal

### Core Meaning

Minimal represents **clarity, restraint, and confidence through simplicity**.

A Minimal brand communicates that the business understands exactly what matters and removes everything else. The visual system stays intentionally quiet so that information, structure, and message take priority.

This style is commonly associated with educational brands, SaaS tools, consultants, and system-driven creators who value clarity over decoration.

Minimal design is not empty. Every element earns its place.

---

### Brand Signal

Minimal communicates:
- Precision over persuasion
- Structure over personality
- Trust through clarity

The brand feels calm, modern, and dependable.

---

### Visual Characteristics

Minimal visuals prioritize:
- High negative space
- Flat or near-flat surfaces
- Very subtle gradients (if any)
- Predictable geometry
- Low visual contrast

Backgrounds feel clean, airy, and neutral. Any shapes used are large, soft, and intentionally unobtrusive.

Texture is either absent or extremely subtle, used only to avoid harsh digital flatness.

---

### Color Logic

In Minimal:
- The primary color is used sparingly
- Secondary and accent colors appear as muted tints
- Most surfaces live in off-whites, soft charcoals, or very light color washes

Color supports hierarchy, not emotion.

---

### Overlay Safety

All Minimal backgrounds are designed to remain text-safe, overlay-friendly, and visually subordinate to foreground content such as text, icons, cutout figures, or screenshots.

---

### Prompt Bias (Internal)

Bias toward:
- Plain or softly graded backgrounds
- Neutral tones
- High negative space
- Smooth, flat surfaces

Avoid:
- Realism or scenery
- Objects or illustrations
- Visual noise or texture

---

### Best For

Minimal is best suited for:
- Educational carousels
- Step-by-step guides
- How-to content
- System diagrams
- Calm, instructional messaging

### Not Ideal For

Minimal is not ideal for:
- Sales-heavy promotions
- High-energy launches
- Emotion-driven storytelling

---

### Mental Model

Design that disappears so the message can speak.

---

## Style 2: Bold

### Core Meaning

Bold represents **confidence, energy, and decisive presence**.

A Bold brand communicates authority and momentum. It is designed to capture attention quickly and make strong visual statements without becoming chaotic.

This style is used when visibility, impact, and differentiation matter.

---

### Brand Signal

Bold communicates:
- Confidence over caution
- Direction over discussion
- Momentum over neutrality

The brand feels assertive, modern, and unmistakable.

---

### Visual Characteristics

Bold visuals prioritize:
- High contrast
- Strong color blocks
- Clear separation between elements
- Directional shapes and layouts

Backgrounds feel powerful and structured. Visual energy is controlled, not random.

Lighting and tonal contrast are more pronounced than in other styles.

---

### Color Logic

In Bold:
- The primary color takes center stage
- Secondary colors are clearly visible
- Accent colors are used deliberately to draw attention

Color is expressive and intentional.

---

### Overlay Safety

Bold backgrounds are designed to support overlays without competing with text or subjects. High contrast areas are placed strategically to preserve readability.

---

### Prompt Bias (Internal)

Bias toward:
- Strong contrast
- Saturated or deep tones
- Structured abstract shapes
- Clear light and dark regions

Avoid:
- Random textures
- Visual clutter
- Playful or decorative elements

---

### Best For

Bold is best suited for:
- Launch announcements
- Promotions
- High-impact educational content
- Strong-opinion creator brands

### Not Ideal For

Bold is not ideal for:
- Long-form reading slides
- Calm instructional sequences
- Subtle or reflective content

---

### Mental Model

Design that leads before the words do.

---

## Style 3: Elegant

### Core Meaning

Elegant represents **refinement, restraint, and premium presence**.

An Elegant brand communicates experience, taste, and authority without urgency. It values balance, proportion, and composure over attention-grabbing tactics.

This style implies maturity and long-term thinking.

---

### Brand Signal

Elegant communicates:
- Experience over urgency
- Taste over trends
- Authority without volume

The brand feels composed, thoughtful, and premium.

---

### Visual Characteristics

Elegant visuals prioritize:
- Balance and proportion
- Soft lighting
- Subtle tonal contrast
- Gentle depth

Backgrounds feel editorial and polished rather than modern-for-modern’s-sake.

Gradients are smooth and layered. Any texture is subtle and refined.

---

### Color Logic

In Elegant:
- Colors are restrained and often muted
- Saturation is lower
- Accents are used sparingly

Primary colors appear as refined tones rather than bright statements.

---

### Overlay Safety

Elegant backgrounds remain text-safe and overlay-friendly, designed to elevate foreground content without distraction.

---

### Prompt Bias (Internal)

Bias toward:
- Soft gradients
- Tonal depth
- Subtle lighting
- Minimal abstract shapes

Avoid:
- Harsh contrast
- Playful shapes
- Trend-driven visuals

---

### Best For

Elegant is best suited for:
- Thought leadership
- Case studies
- Premium services
- Strategy and advisory content

### Not Ideal For

Elegant is not ideal for:
- Flash promotions
- Youthful or playful brands
- High-energy launch moments

---

### Mental Model

Design that signals authority without asking for attention.

---

## How to Choose a Style

Choosing the right style helps ensure your content looks intentional, consistent, and aligned with your brand’s goals.

- Choose **Minimal** when the goal is clarity, teaching, or structured information.
- Choose **Bold** when the goal is attention, momentum, or making a strong statement.
- Choose **Elegant** when the goal is authority, trust, or premium positioning.

If you are unsure which style to use, default to **Minimal**.

---

## Style Consistency Over Time

For strongest brand recognition:

- Brands should aim to use **one primary style** consistently across most content.
- Secondary styles may be used intentionally for special cases such as launches, announcements, or premium content.
- Frequent switching between styles can weaken visual identity and brand recall.

Consistency compounds trust.

---

## Style and Color Palette Interaction

Style and color palette work together, but they serve different roles:

- **Style** controls structure, contrast, composition, and visual behavior.
- **Palette** controls color selection and tonal direction.

When conflicts arise, **style takes precedence over palette**. For example, Bold will remain high-contrast even when paired with muted brand colors, while Elegant will remain restrained even with brighter palettes.

Hierarchy:

Style → Palette → Content

---

## What These Styles Are Not

These style presets are intentionally limited.

They are not:
- Illustration styles
- Photography styles
- Scene or environment generators
- Decorative artwork systems

They are designed specifically to generate **supportive, text-safe backgrounds** for slides and overlays.

---

## System-Level Rules (Applies to All Styles)

- All generated backgrounds must remain secondary to foreground content.
- Outputs must remain abstract and non-representational.
- Color palettes selected in the Brand Profile must always be respected.
- Styles should remain predictable and consistent over time.

---

## Extensibility and Future Compatibility

These style presets are designed to scale.

They are intentionally compatible with:
- Additional future styles
- Motion or animation layers
- Video-based backgrounds
- Advanced layout and composition systems

All future styles should follow the same structural framework defined in this document to maintain system coherence.
